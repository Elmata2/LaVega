A Dutch income tax return goes faster when you have collected the data before you log in. This page is a checklist for the return over tax year 2026, which you file in spring 2027. It is preparation, not a step-by-step guide to filling in the form.

## What is known about the dates, and what is not

At the time of writing we found no checklist or dates for the return over 2026 at the Belastingdienst (the Dutch tax authority). The checklist below is therefore based on the latest published version: "Gegevens bij belastingaangifte 2025", for the return you filed in 2026.

The Belastingdienst's extension page (which names no year) gives these usual dates: you can request an extension until 1 May, which gives you until 1 September. You can only request an extension if you received a filing letter (aangiftebrief). With an extension you usually still pay tax interest. The dates for the return over 2026 may follow a similar pattern, but that is not a published fact. Check belastingdienst.nl in spring 2027.

The official pages are in Dutch. Terms are given below in both languages.

## The checklist

These are the items from the Belastingdienst checklist for 2025. For 2026 the list may change in details; replace the years with 2026.

### Personal

- The citizen service number (bsn) of you, your partner and your children.
- Your bank account number.
- Your DigiD, the Dutch digital login, and your partner's.

### Income

- Your annual income statements (jaaropgaven) for the tax year. If you do not have them, payslips can stand in.
- Partner alimony received.

### Bank accounts, savings and investments

- The yearly overview of your current account, your savings account (including your children's) and your investments.
- The yearly overview of your crypto exchange, if you own crypto.
- Details of loans and debts.

These yearly overviews are also the source for box 3. How box 3 works and which reference date applies to your balances is in [How does box 3 work?](/en/questions/how-does-box-3-work). If you have box 3 assets, the Belastingdienst also asks for the data you need to report your actual return.

### Home

- The WOZ value (official property value). For the return over 2025 that is the value with reference date 1 January 2024, found on last year's WOZ notice. The reference date moves each year, so check your notice for the value that applies to your return.
- The annual mortgage statement (jaaropgaaf hypotheek).
- If you bought or sold a home: the notary's settlement statement.

### Deductible items

- Receipts for donations.
- Medical costs that were not reimbursed.
- Partner alimony paid.
- Annuity premiums paid and an overview of disability insurance (AOV) premiums.
- Dividend details.

According to the checklist you cannot deduct the Zvw own contribution, your health insurance premium or your deductible (eigen risico).

### Only if it applies

- The provisional assessment for income tax and the income-related Zvw contribution for the tax year.

## Allowances (toeslagen)

Allowances such as rent or healthcare allowance are not on the income tax checklist; they run through a separate application with Belastingdienst/Toeslagen. The income and assets you report there may relate to your return. Check toeslagen.nl for the data that applies.

## Collecting it during the year

Many documents only become available in January or February, when employers, banks and brokers publish annual statements. You can start earlier:

- create a folder where you save documents as they arrive;
- keep track of donations and medical costs as they happen;
- note your balances per account, so you do not have to hunt through every bank during filing season.

An overview of all accounts and investments in one place helps. LaVega is one such overview: it brings accounts and investments together, read-only. It does not fill in your return or calculate your tax; it shows what you have.

## A first step this week

Create the folder, drop in your mortgage details and WOZ notice, and note when your employer sends the annual statement. That is enough for now.
