## What is box 3?

If you live in the Netherlands, your income tax return has three "boxes". Box 3 is the one for savings and investments. It does not look at the interest or dividends you actually received. It looks at what you owned on 1 January and applies an assumed return to it. That is the deemed (forfaitair) return.

Below is how it works in 2026, as the Dutch tax authority (Belastingdienst) describes it. We checked the figures on 26 October 2026. The percentages for bank balances and debts are still provisional.

Dutch sources use Dutch terms. The ones you will meet: *heffingsvrij vermogen* (tax-free allowance), *fiscale partner* (fiscal partner), *aangifte* (tax return), *peildatum* (reference date).

## The figures for 2026

| Item | 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Deemed return on bank balances | 1.28% (provisional) |
| Deemed return on investments and other assets | 6.00% (fixed) |
| Deemed return on debts | 2.70% (provisional) |
| Tax-free allowance | 59,357 euro per person, 118,714 euro for fiscal partners |
| Rate | 36% |

According to the Belastingdienst, the final percentages for bank balances and debts are set in early 2027 and apply to the final 2026 income tax assessment.

## How is the tax worked out?

The Belastingdienst describes six steps. The reference date is 1 January 2026.

1. **Taxable return.** The fixed percentage per category is applied to your assets and debts on 1 January. Bank balances and investments are added up, then the return on deductible debts is subtracted.
2. **Return base.** Your assets minus deductible debts. Debts only count above a threshold of 3,800 euro per person (7,600 euro for fiscal partners).
3. **Savings and investment base.** The return base minus the tax-free allowance.
4. **Share.** The savings and investment base divided by the return base.
5. **Benefit from savings and investments.** The taxable return times that share. This is your box 3 income.
6. **Tax.** 36% of that benefit.

### A worked example

This is our own calculation following the steps above, rounded and using the provisional percentage. It is not an assessment.

Take one person with 150,000 euro in savings accounts on 1 January 2026 and no debts.

- Taxable return: 1.28% of 150,000 = 1,920 euro.
- Savings and investment base: 150,000 minus 59,357 = 90,643 euro.
- Share: 90,643 / 150,000 = about 60.43%.
- Benefit: 1,920 times 60.43% = about 1,160 euro.
- Tax: 36% of 1,160 = about 418 euro.

If your return base is below the tax-free allowance, the savings and investment base is zero.

## What if your real return differs?

The deemed return is an average, not a measurement of your own return. The Belastingdienst writes that you can report your actual return. For 2025 you do that in the 2025 return. For 2017 to 2024 there is a separate form, the *Opgaaf werkelijk rendement*. Whether and how this works for 2026 we could not establish; see the Belastingdienst.

## The planned move to actual returns

The government wants to change box 3 into a tax on the return actually earned. This is what Rijksoverheid says on the timeline we read:

- The bill, the Wet werkelijk rendement box 3, was sent to the Tweede Kamer on 19 May 2025.
- The Tweede Kamer passed the bill on 12 February 2026.
- The aim is to start on 1 January 2028.
- The latest entry on the timeline is 19 June 2026: a letter to parliament on possible improvements. According to Rijksoverheid the current bill remains the basis for box 3 from 2028. The government is looking at changes that may be included in the 2027 Tax Plan.

What happens in the meantime, we leave out: for the Eerste Kamer (Senate) and later changes we found no official source to cite. Until a new law starts, the system above applies.
