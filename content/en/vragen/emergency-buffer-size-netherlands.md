## The short answer

No single amount applies to everyone. Nibud, the Dutch National Institute for Family Finance Information, writes on its website that how much buffer you need differs per household. This page reports what Nibud publishes. It is Nibud's guidance, not LaVega's.

Nibud's pages are in Dutch. The quotes below are our translations.

## What is a buffer for?

Nibud describes it this way: with a buffer you can pay unexpected, larger and necessary expenses directly when they occur. Think of a broken washing machine or a repair.

Nibud draws a line here. If you also want money set aside for falling or fluctuating income, you need to save for that separately. That question sits outside the buffer Nibud is talking about. This is relevant to irregular income, for example on a fixed-term contract or as a freelancer.

## How does Nibud work out the size?

Nibud has the **BufferBerekenaar** (buffer calculator). The tool calculates a personal buffer, which Nibud calls the Adviesbuffer, based on your household situation. You can find the tool on [nibud.nl](https://www.nibud.nl/tools/een-financiele-buffer/).

The calculation is personal. A number from an article or an acquaintance says little about your household. The Nibud page itself gives no fixed euro amount.

## The ten percent guideline

On building a buffer, Nibud tells every household to set aside at least ten percent of monthly income to build a buffer and keep it topped up. Nibud calls this a guideline: "De tien procent is een richtlijn" ("the ten percent is a guideline").

Nibud notes it can be less in some cases, for example if you receive special assistance for appliances, get second-hand furniture or travel little. The exceptions and the exact wording are on the Nibud page [Sparen, zo pak je het aan](https://www.nibud.nl/onderwerpen/sparen/sparen-zo-pak-je-het-aan/).

Keep two things apart:

- the **size** of the buffer, which differs per household;
- the **build-up**, for which Nibud names the ten percent guideline of monthly income.

## Buffer and other savings goals

A buffer is one purpose among several. Money for a holiday, a move or a new car has a different purpose and a different date. Nibud's calculator is about the buffer for unexpected, larger and necessary expenses. Keeping the purposes apart in your own overview makes it clear which balance belongs to which question. It also shows how much of your savings is already reserved for something else.

## Other rules you see online

You will often read rules such as "three to six months of fixed costs". Those come from general blogs and not from the Nibud pages we read. We do not repeat them here.

## What can you check yourself?

These questions help build an overview, without producing an amount:

- How much money is in accounts you can reach quickly right now?
- Which large costs can be predicted, such as yearly premiums or maintenance?
- Is there room in your monthly budget to set money aside? How much is for you to decide.

An overview of your accounts and a forecast of your cash position make these questions easier to answer. See [What is a personal CFO?](/en/questions/what-is-a-personal-cfo) for that way of looking at money.

## What LaVega does here

LaVega brings your accounts into one overview, builds a forecast of your cash position and signals when your balance drops under a line you set yourself. LaVega does not set the size of your buffer.
