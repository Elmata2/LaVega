## What does a CFO do at a company?

CFO stands for chief financial officer. At a company, the CFO keeps watch over four things:

- **Overview.** How much money is there, in which accounts, and what is the total position?
- **Cash flow.** What comes in over the next weeks and months, and what goes out?
- **Buffer.** Is there enough liquidity to absorb a setback?
- **Goals and fixed costs.** What is planned, and which costs keep running without anyone looking at them?

A CFO does not decide alone. The job is to make sure the people who decide have the right numbers in front of them.

## What does that mean for a household?

A household has the same four questions, without a finance department. That is where the phrase "personal CFO" comes from. In practice:

**Overview.** If you moved to the Netherlands, you may have a Dutch bank account, a foreign account you kept, a savings account, maybe a brokerage account and a pension through an employer. The full picture is nowhere. A personal CFO puts it in one number.

**Cash flow.** Your salary arrives monthly, but the tax bill, the yearly insurance premium and the holiday arrive irregularly. Cash flow means looking at when money comes in and goes out, not only at today's balance.

**Buffer.** How much room do you have if the washing machine breaks or your income dips for a while? For Nibud's published guidance on buffers, read [How big is an emergency buffer in the Netherlands?](/en/questions/emergency-buffer-size-netherlands).

**Goals.** A renovation, a child, a sabbatical. A goal has an amount and a date. An overview shows what is already in place and what is missing.

**Recurring costs.** Subscriptions and fixed costs keep running. They only stand out when you put them in one list. See [How to clean up your subscriptions](/en/guides/clean-up-your-subscriptions).

## What can software do?

Software is good at work that is dull and precise:

- combining transactions from several banks;
- sorting transactions into categories;
- recognising recurring payments;
- calculating your balance a number of weeks ahead if everything keeps running;
- showing when your buffer is about to drop under a line you set yourself.

This is arithmetic on your own numbers. It produces an overview and a forecast, not a verdict.

## What can software not do?

A personal CFO in software does not replace a financial adviser. The limits:

- **Software does not know your full situation.** An inheritance, a planned career move or a growing family are not in your transactions.
- **A forecast is not a guarantee.** A forecast extends what happened in the past. An unexpected bill is not in it.
- **Choices about products need a conversation with a licensed adviser.** Which investments, which mortgage or which insurance fits your situation is a matter for a licensed adviser (AFM licence in the Netherlands).
- **Software does not decide for you.** A good tool shows the numbers and leaves the choice to you.

## A personal CFO without software

The method works on paper or in a spreadsheet. Once a month, take half an hour:

1. Add up all balances.
2. Write down the large payments of the next three months.
3. Check whether the balance after those payments stays above a floor you chose.
4. Go through the fixed costs.

Software takes over the manual work. The questions stay the same.

This is information, not advice.
