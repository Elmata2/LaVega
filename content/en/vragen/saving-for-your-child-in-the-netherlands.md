## What are the options?

If you want to set money aside for a child, there are several routes. This page describes them and lists the facts that play a role in each. Which route suits you is your choice. We name no banks, funds or providers.

If you are an expat, one thing is worth knowing: the rules below are Dutch tax rules, in Dutch sources. Where we cite the Belastingdienst, the pages are in Dutch and the quotes are our translation.

## 1. A savings account in the child's name or a parent's name

This is the simplest route: an account where you put money for later.

Facts to know:

- **Terms differ per account.** Who may withdraw, until what age the money is locked and whether a block applies are set in the provider's terms.
- **The name on the account is not the only thing that counts for box 3.** The Belastingdienst writes in its guide for 2025 that you also report the assets of children who are under 18 on 1 January and over whom you or your fiscal partner have parental authority. If the parents are not fiscal partners, each parent reports half. Check the page for the current year.
- **Return.** For bank balances box 3 uses a deemed return of 1.28% in 2026 (provisional). See [How does box 3 work?](/en/questions/how-does-box-3-work).
- **No market risk.** The balance does not move with markets, but purchasing power can change through inflation.

## 2. An investment account

This route can place money in markets, for example through an account in a parent's name or, depending on the provider, in the child's name.

Facts to know:

- **Value can fall as well as rise.** A return is not guaranteed and you can get back less than you put in.
- **Costs.** Providers charge costs, for example per transaction or per year. They differ per provider and are in their fee schedules.
- **Terms for minors differ.** Not every provider offers accounts for minors, and the rules on who decides differ.
- **Box 3.** For investments and other assets box 3 uses a deemed return of 6.00% in 2026, as the Belastingdienst describes it. The return you actually earn can be higher or lower.

## 3. Gifting to your child

You can also give money, after which it belongs to the child. Tax-free limits exist for this.

The Belastingdienst gives for 2026:

- **Annual exemption for a child: 6,908 euro.** A foster child or stepchild counts as a child.
- **One-time increased exemption, freely spendable: up to 33,129 euro.** Conditions: the child is 18 to 40 at the time of the gift (the 40th birthday counts too) and has not used an increased exemption from you before.
- **One-time increased exemption for an expensive study: up to 69,009 euro**, for a child aged 18 to 40. Separate conditions apply, on the Belastingdienst page.
- The increased exemption takes the place of the annual exemption in that year.
- The increased exemption specifically for a home no longer exists since 1 January 2024. The freely spendable amount can still go towards a home.

Also:

- A gift is a transfer. The money belongs to the child afterwards, and from then on the rules for the child's assets apply.
- Gifts above the exemption fall under gift tax rules. Those are on belastingdienst.nl.

## The options side by side, in facts

Gifting is not a third kind of account: it settles whose money it is, not where it sits.

| | Savings account | Investment account | Gifting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Value moves with markets | No | Yes | Depends on where the money goes |
| Box 3 deemed return, 2026 | 1.28% (provisional) | 6.00% | Not applicable to the gift itself |
| Costs | Per provider | Per provider | None; no gift tax within the exemption |
| Who decides about the money | Per the terms | Per the terms | The child, after the gift |
