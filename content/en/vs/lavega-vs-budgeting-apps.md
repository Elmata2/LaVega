"Budgeting app" is a broad term. Some are part of a bank, others sit outside your bank and read along. This page describes neutrally what bunq's budgeting features do, what LaVega does and where they differ. It is not an assessment and there is no ranking.

We write nothing here about several other well-known apps, because we could not check their current details on their own websites on 26 October 2026.

## Two kinds of tool

**Budgets inside a bank.** You move money, or have your salary split, inside the same bank. The budget works on the accounts you have there.

**An overview across providers.** You pull data from different banks and brokers together and look ahead, without moving money.

These are different starting points, and they do not exclude each other.

## What bunq says about its budgeting features

According to bunq on 26 October 2026 (its budgeting page), bunq offers, among other things:

- **Category accounts**: a separate bank account per category, each with its own IBAN.
- **Income Organizer**: sends incoming payments and salary to the right accounts automatically.
- **Spending limits**: set a limit on each budgeting pot and get an alert before you reach it.
- **Auto Round Up**: rounds card payments up to the nearest euro and saves the difference.
- **Spending trends**: tracks spending by category and can connect external accounts.
- **Invoice scanning**, **notes and receipts** on transactions, **joint account budgeting** and **monthly statements**.

Personal plan prices according to bunq on that date: bunq Free: free; bunq Core: €3.99 per month; bunq Pro: €9.99 per month; bunq Elite: €18.99 per month. The page does not say which budgeting features belong to which plan; check bunq's website for that.

## What LaVega does

According to the description on [lavega.dev](https://www.lavega.dev/en) (26 October 2026):

- Accounts at any bank and investments (Trading 212 and Interactive Brokers) in one overview.
- Import bank exports, or link a bank or broker read-only.
- Automatic categorisation of transactions.
- A forecast of your cash position and a heads-up when your buffer gets tight.
- Finds subscriptions in your transactions and compares savings rates.
- Read-only: no payments or orders.
- Your transactions are encrypted in your browser. LaVega keeps the keys, so technically it can access the data.

LaVega is in beta; no price has been published yet.

## Where they differ

| | A bank's budgeting features (bunq) | LaVega |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Works across | Accounts within the bank, plus linked external accounts | Accounts at any bank and linked brokers |
| Moving money | Yes, it is a bank account | No, read-only |
| Budget pots and limits | Yes, according to bunq | Not described on lavega.dev |
| Cash position forecast | Not mentioned on the budgeting page | Yes, according to lavega.dev |
| Investments in the overview | Not mentioned on the budgeting page | Trading 212 and Interactive Brokers |

"Not mentioned" only means it was not on the page we read, not that the feature does not exist.

## A question to ask yourself

Do you want to organise money in practical pots inside one bank, or mainly want an overview and a look ahead across several providers? That decides which type of tool fits your situation. You can also use both.
