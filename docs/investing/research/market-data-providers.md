# Research: market-data / price providers

_Resolves [#18](https://github.com/Elmata2/LaVega/issues/18). Read `docs/CONTEXT.md` and `docs/investing/CONNECTORS.md` first — brokers give positions and trades, never a price series, so portfolio-value-over-time and benchmark overlays need an external source. This doc finds and licenses that source; it does not build it._

## Method

Every claim below is sourced from the provider's own pricing, docs, or terms-of-service page (linked inline). Where a primary ToS page returned a PDF that couldn't be parsed cleanly (Alpha Vantage) or a page that 403's to automated fetches (Financial Modeling Prep's pricing page), the provider's own support/FAQ/docs pages or its own policy sub-pages are used instead and flagged as such — never a third-party blog's paraphrase of licensing terms. General "how do these APIs compare" summaries from review sites were _not_ used for any licensing claim.

## Licensing landmines — the loud call-outs

**1. Financial Modeling Prep — the free/personal tier's license text appears to forbid the self-hosted default too, not just a hosted tier.** FMP looks attractive on paper: cheap, broad EU-exchange coverage claims, and a dedicated ISIN search endpoint the ticket explicitly wanted. But FMP's Terms of Service state the personal-use license "may only be used by a customer who is an individual, strictly for personal, non-business and non-commercial use," is "personal to the customer, who may not... integrate the Data or Services into any tools or applications accessible by third parties, or use the Services to host, share, display, or provide content for others," and that "displaying or redistributing data sourced from FMP requires a specific Data Display and Licensing Agreement." Read literally, an AGPL open-source app that _other people_ download and self-host is "a tool or application accessible by third parties" — this is not obviously satisfied just because each self-hoster brings their own key. Do not wire FMP into any code path LaVega ships without first getting written confirmation from FMP that self-hosted-OSS-with-a-user-supplied-key qualifies as personal use. ([FMP Terms of Service](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/terms-of-service))

**2. Yahoo Finance (`yfinance` / scraping unofficial endpoints) — the best free EU+FX+indices coverage found is also the one explicitly barred by the source's own terms.** Yahoo shut down its official public finance API in 2017; everything since is reverse-engineered. Yahoo's Terms of Service prohibit "automated means, devices, programs, algorithms or methodologies, including... robots, spiders, scrapers, data mining tools" without express written permission, prohibit reuse "for any commercial purpose" without permission, and prohibit using Yahoo content to "create any database, archive... data feed" that competes with or substitutes for Yahoo's own services. ([Yahoo ToS](https://legal.yahoo.com/us/en/yahoo/terms/otos/index.html)) The `yfinance` library's own README/disclaimer concurs: it is "not affiliated, endorsed, or vetted by Yahoo," and "the Yahoo! finance API is intended for personal use only." Wiring this in as LaVega's silent default would put every self-hoster in violation of Yahoo's ToS on day one, and it is technically fragile besides (undocumented endpoints, informal IP-based rate limiting/blocking). This is the exact same risk shape `CONNECTORS.md` already names for DeGiro — treat it the same way if it's used at all: an explicitly-labeled, opt-in, consent-gated fallback, never the wired-in default.

**3. Polygon.io (rebranded **Massive.com**, 30 Oct 2025) is disqualified outright, at any price.** It is confirmed **US-only** for equities, options, forex, and indices — no Euronext, XETRA, or LSE coverage exists at any tier, despite the rebrand's "scale" and "comprehensive coverage" language. Included in this doc only because the ticket named it explicitly; not viable for LaVega's core European-equities requirement. ([pricing, now hosted at massive.com after a 301 redirect from polygon.io](https://massive.com/pricing))

**4. Twelve Data's generous-looking free tier (800 credits/day) does not cover Europe.** The free "Basic" plan is scoped to exactly 3 markets — US equities, forex, crypto — and Twelve Data's own per-exchange pages show Euronext Paris and Xetra gated behind the paid **Grow** tier (~€66–79/mo). A self-hoster on the free key alone cannot serve LaVega's stated European-coverage requirement with Twelve Data.

**5. Tiingo's paid "commercial" tier is not a redistribution license.** Even the $499/yr Business plan is explicitly "Internal Use Only" — paying more doesn't unlock the hosted-tier use case; that still needs a separate conversation with Tiingo.

**6. Stooq and unauthenticated-Yahoo-style sources share a risk class worth naming once, generally:** no official API, no clearly published data-licensing terms, CAPTCHA/rate-limit walls that exist specifically to block automation, and no support contract to fall back on when something breaks. Fine as a manual, personal, occasional-use fallback; not something to make a redistributed open-source project depend on, and never viable for a paid hosted tier.

## Data volume implications (feeds the storage-seam ticket)

~40 positions + 1 benchmark = 41 price series. At daily EOD granularity over 5 years (~1,260 trading days), that's roughly 41 × 1,260 ≈ 51,660 rows, each with a handful of numeric fields (O/H/L/C/volume) — on the order of ~300K numbers total. Trivial for SQLite/IndexedDB/Postgres; this does not meaningfully constrain the storage seam.

Request-budget implications differ sharply by provider, because most return a symbol's **entire history in one call**:

- A one-time backfill of 41 symbols costs ~41 API calls; a daily incremental update costs another ~41/day (fewer if a provider supports batch requests).
- **Alpha Vantage (25/day) and EODHD free (20/day)** cannot even complete a one-time 41-symbol backfill in a single day, let alone sustain daily updates.
- **marketstack free (100/month)** would burn most of a month's quota on one backfill alone, leaving nothing for daily updates — effectively unusable free.
- **Twelve Data free (800/day), Finnhub free (60/min), Tiingo free (1,000/day), FMP free (250/day)** all have plenty of _rate_ headroom for this workload — but see the coverage/licensing caveats above before any of them qualify.

## Recommendation, split by tier

**Self-hosted tier (default, local-first, must work on a self-supplied key) — the honest finding: no provider researched combines (adequate EU-equity coverage) + (5-year daily history) + (a genuinely free, clean-license key) at once.** Every option that's actually free and covers Europe (Yahoo/`yfinance`, Stooq) is unofficial and either explicitly ToS-prohibited or has no clear data-license at all; every provider with confirmed multi-year European EOD coverage and clean personal-use licensing (EODHD, Twelve Data, marketstack Basic) requires a small paid key.

- **Recommended default: EODHD "EOD Historical Data — All World" (€19.99/mo, self-funded by the self-hoster with their own card/key).** It's the cheapest option with _confirmed_ non-US EOD coverage back to ~2000, FX and index data, 100,000 calls/day (plenty for the ~41-symbol daily workload), and it bundles the ISIN/CUSIP/FIGI **ID Mapping API** in the same subscription — solving the instrument-identification problem the ticket flagged, in the same plan. Personal/Non-Professional licensing terms are compatible with a self-hoster running LaVega for their own portfolio (local storage during the active subscription, delete on cancellation, no resale). This is "free of LaVega's involvement" in the local-first sense that matters (BYO key, no LaVega account, no LaVega infrastructure in the path) even though it isn't literally $0.
- **FX leg specifically: use Frankfurter/ECB regardless of which equity provider is chosen.** It's genuinely free, keyless, unlimited, and open-source — no reason to spend an equity-data provider's request budget or accept its licensing terms just for currency conversion.
- **ISIN → instrument bridge: use OpenFIGI as the neutral layer, decoupled from the price vendor.** Resolve each broker's ISIN to a FIGI/ticker/exchange once via OpenFIGI (free, MIT), cache that mapping locally, then query whichever price vendor is configured using the resolved ticker. This means the instrument-ID problem doesn't need to be re-solved if the price vendor is ever swapped.
- **A true-$0 fallback (e.g., Yahoo/`yfinance`) may still be offered, but only the way `CONNECTORS.md` already treats DeGiro:** explicitly labeled unofficial/highest-risk, opt-in, gated behind the same one-time risk-disclosure consent pattern, never the silent default.

**Hosted paid tier (future, LaVega may proxy/cache and serve data to multiple end-users) — needs an explicit redistribution license, not just an API key; this is a real "two different providers" situation the ticket predicted.**

- **marketstack Professional ($49.99/mo)** has the most naturally-fitting license text of the group: it explicitly contemplates a licensee's application displaying data to _the app's own end-users_ for those end-users' personal use, without a separate redistribution add-on, as long as end-users don't further store/redistribute it themselves. That maps closely onto "LaVega shows each hosted customer their own portfolio's prices." Still worth a direct written confirmation from marketstack before relying on it at that scale.
- **Twelve Data's Redistribution Rights Add-on / White Label** is the vendor's purpose-built answer to exactly this shape of problem, and Twelve Data also has the strongest EU coverage of the group at its Pro tier (real-time Cboe Europe data, 70+ markets) — worth a sales conversation if the hosted tier needs real-time EU pricing eventually, not just EOD.
- **EODHD's "Professional User" path** (prior written approval for redistribution) is a third option, on top of the All-World plan already recommended for the self-hosted tier — potentially the simplest to negotiate since the base subscription is already in place.
- **Do not use FMP or Finnhub for the hosted tier without their explicit written commercial/redistribution agreement** — both have ToS text drafted narrowly enough to exclude embedding in any third-party-facing product by default (see landmines #1 and Finnhub's "Personal plan can't be used by any business even internally without written approval").
